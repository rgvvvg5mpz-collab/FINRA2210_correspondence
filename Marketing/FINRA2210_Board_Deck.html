Board briefing · Compliance technology

A first-pass reviewer for FINRA Rule 2210 that runs on a laptop

A fine-tuned 3.8-billion-parameter model reads a broker-dealer email, text, post or web page and returns what a principal needs first: the communication type, a compliant or not-compliant verdict, each violation with its rule and the exact words, the severity, and next steps.

In

One message as sent, plus who receives it: channel, sender, recipient type and the number of recipients in the last 30 days.

→
Reviewer

Phi-4-mini-instruct 3.8B with a 185 MB LoRA adapter, on the firm’s own laptop.

→
Out

correspondence · not compliant · 2210(d)(1)(B) with the quoted words, severity and next steps.

Executive summary1 / 10

What it does

Every message gets a classification, a verdict and a cited reason in seconds

TEXT MESSAGE · registered rep, Harbor Point Securities LLC
To: 3 retail clients · trailing 30 days: 3
Hi all, the Atlas Income Fund is a can’t-lose 7% a year. Want in? – Dana
Model verdict (actual output)
correspondencenot compliantseverity: high
promissory_or_guarantee · 2210(d)(1)(B)

Excerpt: the Atlas Income Fund is a can’t-lose 7% a year. A fixed annual return for a mutual fund is a promissory claim. Next: escalate to the supervising principal; send a corrective text to all three recipients.

Classify

Correspondence, retail or institutional, from who receives it: 25 or fewer retail recipients in 30 days is correspondence.

Flag and cite

Any of 23 violation types in 5 groups, each tied to one rule paragraph, with the offending words quoted verbatim.

Route

Severity from none to high and one to three concrete next steps, so the principal starts from a draft review, not a blank page.

The principal still adds one finding: no member firm name, 2210(d)(3). Absent items are the model’s weakest spot.

Where it stands

Fine-tunedUntrained base

Strong

  • 95.3% verdicts on WhatsApp, voicemail, options, crypto and Spanish messages it never saw.
  • 99.8% right on the communication type.
  • Right group of problem: F1 0.92.

Not yet

  • Exact category: F1 0.80 (test), 0.69 (unfamiliar).
  • Misses absent items, such as the firm name.
  • Labels not yet reviewed by a compliance expert.

Proposed next steps

  • Expert label review on a sample, starting with the disagreements.
  • v0.2 data round: 2,400 chat messages and targeted examples for weak categories.
  • Supervised pilot with principals, logging every accept, edit and reject.
  • Release gate: no version ships unless it beats the last one.

Decisions needed: pilot scope, who reviews the labels, repository access and licensing.

Improvement and governance

Every new version earns its release against a fixed yardstick

STAGE 1

Reviewer feedback

Accept, edit, reject logged with the model version

→
STAGE 2

Label audit

Claude Opus rules on model-vs-label disagreements

→
STAGE 3

Supervised round

Retrain from base on corrected data

→
STAGE 4

Preference training

DPO on the model’s own mistakes

→
STAGE 5

Reinforcement

GRPO with a reward computed from labels

→
STAGE 6

Release gate

Ship only if no check gets worse

Controls

  • A registered principal makes every decision.
  • Each review records the model version.
  • Test and OOD sets never enter training.
  • Review runs locally; messages stay on firm hardware.

Roadmap

  • v0.2 audited labels, chat top-up, targeted examples
  • v0.3 preference training on v0.2’s errors
  • v0.4 reinforcement, if the gate shows a need
  • Ongoing pilot feedback feeds every round

Open risks

  • Labels are synthetic until experts review them.
  • Rules change; the digest reflects October 2026.
  • Scope is the text: no filing deadlines or workflow.
  • Exact-category naming is the main accuracy gap.
